Interior Design Cost Guide

Insights | May 10, 2026

Planning Your Budget Smartly

One of the most frequent questions homeowners ask is, "How much will my home interiors cost?" Setting up a realistic budget requires understanding the key financial factors: square footage, grade of plywood, choice of surface finishes, and hardware quality. Planning ahead prevents unexpected budget overruns.

1. Primary Material Grades

The core material constitutes the largest portion of your budget. For wet zones like modular kitchens or washrooms, BWR (Boiling Water Resistant) marine-grade plywood is essential, costing slightly more than standard Commercial Plywood. Standard bedrooms or living room paneling can comfortably use MR (Moisture Resistant) plywood or high-density fiberboards (HDF), which optimizes costs.

2. Finishing Surfaces

The outer finish dictates both style and cost:

  • Laminates: The most budget-friendly, durable option. Glossy or matte sheets are easy to clean and maintain.
  • Acrylics: Yields a hyper-reflective glass-like finish, ideal for modern kitchens, but commands a higher premium.
  • Veneers & PU Paint: Real wood sheets finished with premium polyurethane (PU) polish provide a luxurious, natural wood feel at the top of the price range.

"Do not compromise on functional hardware. Cabinets can easily be repainted or polished, but cheap hinges will warp within two seasons."

3. Hardware and Accessories

The moving parts of your home are critical. Settle for premium, soft-close hardware brands like Ebco, Hettich, or Hafele. While these add to the initial project invoice, they guarantee a lifespan of 10-15 years, saving significant maintenance expenses down the road.

4. Custom vs. Readymade Costing

Custom built-in wardrobes and TV consoles are built exactly to your room measurements, sealing all gaps and maximizing storage. Readymade standing furniture is cheaper initially but lacks custom styling, space optimization, and durability, often needing replacement much sooner.
